## 1. The business in one page

**What it is.** A small digital studio that builds and runs the online presence of local
businesses: websites, social media, Google Business Profiles, advertising, branding and simple
automation.

**Who it serves.** Cafés, salons, tradespeople, independent shops and similar businesses with
roughly 1–20 staff. They are busy, they know they "should be online", and they don’t have time to
do it well.

**How it makes money.** Two kinds of income:

1. **One-off projects** (websites, setups, branding): bigger payments that fund the early months.
2. **Monthly retainers** (care plans, social management, ads management, Google profile
   management, automation upkeep): smaller payments that build into a stable monthly income.

**The goal for year one:** reach about **£3,000–£4,500 a month in retainers** while selling
**one or two website projects a month**. That level supports a full-time income for one person.

**Why it can work.** Plenty of local businesses still have no website or an unclaimed or
incomplete Google profile. The UK government’s business data survey puts the share with no
website at roughly 22–35%, depending on the year and business size. Most of these owners won’t
learn to do it themselves, and big agencies are too expensive for them.

## 2. How the business works day to day

### The customer journey

| Step             | What happens                                                                                    | Who does what                |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| 1. Lead          | A business owner finds you through outreach, a referral, Google or social media                 | You                          |
| 2. Free check-up | You review their website, Google listing and social pages and send three practical improvements | You, about 45 minutes        |
| 3. Call          | A 30-minute chat about their business and goals                                                 | You and the owner            |
| 4. Quote         | A written, itemised quote with one-off work and monthly services listed separately              | You, within 2 working days   |
| 5. Deposit       | 50% upfront for projects; monthly services paid in advance by direct debit                      | Owner                        |
| 6. Build         | Discover → Create → Launch, with drafts approved by the owner                                   | You (plus freelancers later) |
| 7. Grow          | Monthly retainer work and a plain-English monthly summary                                       | You                          |
| 8. Review        | Every 3 months: what’s working, what to add, a natural point to upsell                          | You and the owner            |

### Rules that protect your time and margin

- **Fixed scope in writing.** Every quote lists what’s included, the number of revision rounds
  (two is normal) and what costs extra.
- **Clients own their accounts.** Domains, Google profiles, ad accounts and social accounts are
  registered to the client, with you added as a manager. It builds trust and avoids disputes.
- **Ad spend goes directly to the platforms** on the client’s card. It never touches your bank
  account, so it never inflates your turnover towards the VAT threshold.
- **Templates for everything:** quote, onboarding checklist, content-approval form, monthly
  report, offboarding checklist.

### Packages to offer when a client asks "what do you recommend?"

- **Get Found** (one-off, about £495–£650): Google profile setup, two social profiles set up,
  review-request QR card. Low price, fast to deliver, and it leads into the larger packages.
- **Launch** (one-off, about £2,400–£3,500): branding starter, 5-page website, Google profile
  and social setup.
- **Grow** (monthly, about £550–£900): care plan, Google profile management and social content.
  Add ads management on top when it makes sense.

**Pricing tips.** Ask for a 50% deposit on projects. Use a 3-month minimum term on retainers,
then make them rolling monthly. Put prices up for new clients every year. Offer your first three
clients a "founding client" discount (around 30%) in return for a written testimonial and
permission to use the work as a real case study.

## 4. Profit margin: what it really looks like

### Margin per service

For a one-person studio the cash costs of each job are tiny. The real cost is **your time**, so
watch two numbers: **gross margin** (price minus direct costs) and **effective hourly rate**
(gross profit divided by hours).

| Service                   | Price   | Direct costs                 | Gross margin | Hours | Effective hourly rate                             |
| ------------------------- | ------- | ---------------------------- | ------------ | ----- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| 5-page website            | £1,800  | ~£60 (fonts, plugins, stock) | 97%          | 30    | ~£58/h                                            |
| Google profile setup      | £245    | £0                           | 100%         | 3.5   | ~£70/h                                            |
| Care plan                 | £60/mo  | ~£20 hosting                 | 67%          | 1.5   | ~£27/h (but it buys loyalty and leads to upsells) |
| Google profile management | £139/mo | £0                           | 100%         | 2.5   | ~£56/h                                            |
| Social management         | £550/mo | ~£15 tools share             | 97%          | 10    | ~£54/h                                            |
| Ads management            | £350/mo | ~£5                          | 99%          | 4     | ~£86/h                                            |
| Automation bundle         | £750    | ~£20 (Zapier or Make share)  | 97%          | 8     | ~£91/h                                            |

For comparison, UK freelance day rates found in the research were about £225–£420 a day
(£30–£55 an hour). Hitting **£50+ an hour** across your work means you’re pricing well.

That is in line with reported small-agency figures of **50–70% gross** and **15–25% net**.

### What "profit margin" means for you

- In scenarios A and B, almost all the profit **is your pay**. An 80% margin sounds high, but it
  is your salary, before income tax and National Insurance.
- Once you hire help, margin drops to the 15–25% typical of agencies. In return, the business can
  grow beyond your own hours.
- **The VAT threshold matters.** Once your taxable turnover passes **£90,000** in any rolling
  12 months, you must register and add 20% VAT. Many small-business clients can’t reclaim VAT, so
  your prices effectively rise by 20% for them. Plan for this before you get there: raise prices
  gradually, or keep ad spend and hosting billed directly to clients.
- **Tax basics:** as a sole trader you pay income tax and Class 4 National Insurance on profit.
  As a limited company, corporation tax is 19% on profits up to £50,000 and 25% above £250,000,
  with marginal relief in between. Ask an accountant which structure suits you once profit passes
  about £30,000–£40,000.

## 5. Marketing plan

### Positioning

> **"You run the business. We build your online presence."**
> A friendly, capable local partner, priced for small businesses, that explains everything in
> plain English.

Compete on **clarity, care and speed**, not on being the cheapest.

### Channels, in order of priority

1. **The free online check-up, delivered in person and by email.** Make a list of 50 local
   businesses with no website, a weak website or an incomplete Google profile. Visit quiet shops
   mid-morning. Leave a printed one-page check-up with three specific fixes and your contact
   details. Aim for 10 check-ups a week. Expect roughly 1 in 10 to become a client in the early
   months; track your real numbers.
2. **Your own Google Business Profile and reviews.** Set it up on day one. Ask every client for
   an honest review. It’s free, and it ranks you for "web design near me".
3. **Referrals.** Offer existing clients a thank-you (for example £100 credit or a free month of
   their care plan) for every referral that becomes a client.
4. **Partnerships.** Accountants, bookkeepers, sign makers, printers, shopfitters, commercial
   landlords, local business improvement districts, chambers of commerce and networking groups
   such as BNI. Offer their clients the free check-up.
5. **Your own Instagram and LinkedIn.** Post before-and-after redesigns (concepts until you have
   real clients), "60-second fixes for your Google listing", and short client stories.
6. **Local Facebook groups.** Answer questions helpfully and follow each group’s rules on
   promotion.
7. **Small paid tests.** £5–£10 a day on Meta, targeting local business owners with the free
   check-up offer. Stop anything that doesn’t produce check-up requests within 2–3 weeks.
8. **Search ads for "web design [town]".** These can be expensive, so test small once your
   portfolio is real.

### First 90 days

| Weeks | Focus                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1–2   | Register the business, open a business bank account, get insurance, pay the ICO fee, set up email, phone and the enquiry form, and put the website live           |
| 3–4   | Build your list of 50 local prospects and create the check-up template and one-page leave-behind                                                                  |
| 5–8   | 10 check-ups a week. Sign 3 founding clients at a discount in return for testimonials and case studies                                                            |
| 9–12  | Replace concept projects on the website with real (approved) work, ask for reviews, start referral and partner outreach, and convert project clients to retainers |

### Numbers to track every week

Check-ups sent → calls booked → quotes sent → clients won · average project value · monthly
recurring revenue · retainer cancellations · hours per client · effective hourly rate.

## 7. Legal and admin checklist

- [ ] Register as a sole trader with HMRC, or form a limited company (£100 online at Companies
      House since February 2026)
- [ ] Open a separate business bank account
- [ ] Pay the ICO data protection fee (£52 a year, tier 1)
- [ ] Buy professional indemnity and public liability insurance
- [ ] Write simple client terms: scope, revisions, payment (50% deposit), ownership of work and
      accounts, cancellation, and liability limits. A solicitor-checked template is worth paying for.
- [ ] Complete the privacy notice on the website (the bracketed details)
- [ ] Use a password manager and never reuse client passwords. Ask clients to add you as a user
      rather than share their passwords.
- [ ] **Never write, buy or incentivise fake reviews.** The Digital Markets, Competition and
      Consumers Act 2024 banned fake reviews from 6 April 2025, with fines of up to 10% of
      turnover.
- [ ] Keep records of income and expenses from day one, ready for Making Tax Digital

## 8. Main risks and how to handle them

| Risk                               | What to do about it                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| You run out of hours               | Standard templates and packages; raise prices; bring in freelancers for overflow             |
| Scope creep                        | Written scope, two revision rounds, and change requests quoted separately                    |
| Clients with very small budgets    | Lead with the "Get Found" package; don’t discount retainers below what they cost you in time |
| Clients cancelling retainers       | Quarterly reviews, a clear monthly summary, and real results reported honestly               |
| Late payment                       | Deposits, direct debit for retainers, and work paused when invoices are overdue              |
| AI tools making basic work cheaper | Sell outcomes and care, not just files; use AI to work faster yourself                       |
| Platform changes (Meta, Google)    | Spread clients across services; keep learning                                                |
